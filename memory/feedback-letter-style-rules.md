Read `kb/BizDev/letter-style.md` before drafting any letter to a real person. It was derived on 2026-08-24 from Alexander's own rewrite of a drafted letter, which cut a third of the words without losing anything, and it is the canonical version. The core, so that a session which never opens the file still holds it:

Say the thing, never announce that you are about to say it: any sentence whose only job is to introduce the next sentences («two things are worth a minute of your time», «let me explain why») is deleted. Build one chain of consequences rather than a request followed by a block of supporting arguments, so the reader is carried instead of having to assemble the case. Do not narrate your own conduct or intentions («I am not going to chase her»). Do not explain to the reader what the reader knows better than you. Do not answer an objection nobody raised, which is the writing-side face of [[feedback-no-saccharine-in-french-letters]]. Frame the request as a contribution to something the reader is already trying to achieve, not as a favour to you. Offer the transferable skill, not only the finished artefact. Pay courtesy once. Quote the smallest true number, and never round the effort down to nothing.

The generating test behind all of it: every sentence must carry a fact, a request, or a consequence of the sentence before it, and a sentence carrying none of the three is deleted. Length is the result of that test, never the target.

**Why:** on 2026-08-24 Alexander rewrote a letter I had drafted and asked me to explain the difference. All of it came from writing to cover the argument rather than to be read: I added a paragraph per imaginable objection and a signpost before each block, each addition locally defensible, the sum twice the necessary length. He then asked for the rules to be written down. Related: [[feedback-cut-slop-keep-depth]], [[feedback-no-em-dash-in-letters]], [[feedback-english-level-for-french-readers]].
