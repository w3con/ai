See a problem, offer to fix it. Say what it is, say what you would do about it, or ask him what to do with it — and stop there. Founding the work is his call, not yours. A card, a plan, a task folder, a branch: none of that gets created because you noticed something.

**Why, 2026-08-30.** The rule used to say the opposite — found the card the moment you notice, never merely propose — and Alex replaced it in his own words: «Поменяй это правило. Увидел проблему, предложи ее починить. Или спроси, что с этим делать. Ну, предложить починить, да, а не заводить работу.» What produced the reversal: he swore at a plan I had handed to an executor, because one of its phases held several phases' worth of work; I turned his complaint into a founded card on the spot, and he asked who had asked me to. Nobody had. His anger about how the current work is going is not a commission for new work, and the older rule had made me read it as one.

**The small-repair floor is unchanged.** A fix that is obvious, small and reversible, inside work you are already doing, that nobody else has to weigh in on, and whose whole story fits in a commit message or one line of a document that already exists — that you simply do, commit on its own so the repair is legible, and name in the final report. This is above all the machinery: a stale path, a wrong flag, a missing entry in a generated index. It was never worth a card and it is not worth a question either.

**What never changes.** Never silently work around a problem. Either it is fixed and recorded, or it is named out loud in the same message where you noticed it — there is no third state where it lives only in your own head. That half of the old rule survives intact; what died is the part that let you convert the observation into work without his word.

**How to apply:** name the problem in one or two sentences, say what you would do about it, and wait. If he says to build it, that sentence is your authorisation and the work starts then.
