Treat a warm, enthusiastic-sounding reaction in a business call ("intéressant", "utile", "génial", "je vais y réfléchir") as background noise, not as evidence that something will actually happen. Log the concrete outputs of a call instead: a name given, a date committed, a document sent. Their absence is the real state of the relationship, no matter how pleasant the conversation felt.

**Why:** on 2026-07-20, working a Validité BizDev contact card, I framed one contact's warm-but-vague call (no names given, no firm date) as a personal trait worth flagging as a reliability risk specific to her. Alexander corrected this: nearly all his business conversations run warm in the moment — he named it as likely a French/general-business-politeness pattern — so reading warmth as a signal about one particular contact's reliability was both inaccurate and unfair to that person. He was explicit that any value in noting this is for how I calibrate future read on business calls, not as a fact to record about the contact.

**How to apply:** when logging or reasoning about any outreach/BizDev/CustDev call, do not write "the call went well" as if it carries evidential weight, and do not attribute a warm-but-non-committal call to one contact's individual character. Note only what was concretely produced (or not) — a name, a date, a document, a next step with an owner — and calibrate expectations off that, not off tone. This applies broadly across contacts and projects, not to a single relationship.
