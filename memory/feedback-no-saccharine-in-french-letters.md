In a letter to a French business contact, cut every line whose job is to make the reader comfortable rather than to carry information. The specific patterns Alex struck out of a draft on 2026-08-15, each of which he called commercial bullshit:

- Permission to refuse — «si le sujet n'est pas prioritaire, dites-le moi franchement, cela ne me vexera pas».
- Reassurance about pressure — «sans vouloir vous presser», «ni visio, ni créneau à caler».
- Coaching-register menu questions — «laquelle des trois vous parle le plus ?».
- Pre-emptive apology for the previous letter's length.
- Anticipating an objection the reader never raised, then answering it.

**Why:** this register is American sales softening in French clothes. It reads either as insecurity or as a translated marketing template, and it signals that the writer is trying to be liked rather than trying to be useful. French professional correspondence is not less polite than this — it is more formal (vouvoiement, a real formule de politesse) while being far more direct about the substance. The politeness lives in the register, never in emotional cushioning.

**How to apply:** keep the formal frame and state the thing in one clear sentence, then stop. Say what you are proposing and what you are asking for. If the reader wants to decline, they know how; writing the escape hatch for them adds nothing and costs the letter its adult tone. Better still, replace the ask with a delivery — send the thing itself rather than an offer to send it, which removes the need for softening entirely.
