Every time an incoming letter is read and something in it is about to be recorded as a finding, first ask two questions about the text itself, and write the answer down next to the quote: was this written by a person or drafted by an AI assistant, and does it contain anything the sender did not already receive from us?

The second question is the one that actually protects the knowledge base. A reply that repeats our own vocabulary, in our own order, and closes with agreement ("you put it perfectly", "exactly as you said") is not independent confirmation of anything, whoever or whatever typed it. It becomes dangerous the moment it is filed as evidence, because a month later nobody remembers that the sender was handed the wording.

The tells worth looking for: the sender's key phrases match phrases from our last message almost word for word; the letter agrees with every point and pushes back on none; there is no concrete case, company, number or date anywhere in it; the structure mirrors our own paragraph order. A separate and weaker tell of assistant drafting is uniformly polished prose from a correspondent whose earlier messages were short and plain.

**Why:** Alex raised this on 2026-08-06 about a reply from an association's project manager. That letter reproduced two theses from Alex's own message, in the same order, and ended with "vous l'avez parfaitement souligné". Read quickly, it looked like the first outside confirmation of our core hypothesis about which companies feel the pain; read against the sent message, it was an echo. Alex's point was that the sender's side may well be answering with an AI assistant too, which makes echo the default outcome rather than the exception.

**How to apply:** when a quote goes into a contact card or an evidence ledger, grade it in the same line — first-hand testimony about the sender's own situation, an observation about others, or agreement with our framing — and say which of our messages preceded it. To get real evidence instead of echo, ask the open question before presenting our version of the problem, never after.
