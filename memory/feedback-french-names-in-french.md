When writing to Alex in Russian, keep French personal names, company names and place names in their original French spelling: `Camille Ernould`, `Éric Mezin`, `Amandine Caux`, `Tourcoing`, `Lille`. Never transliterate them into Cyrillic (`Камий Эрну`, `Эрик Мезен`, `Туркуэн`).

**Why:** Alex asked for this directly on 2026-08-04 («когда пишешь мне французские имена, пиши их на французском»). A transliteration is a guess at pronunciation that he then has to reverse-engineer back into the spelling he needs to type into an email client, a calendar entry or a search box. The French form is the one that actually matches the contact cards, the mail headers and the outgoing letters, so it is the only form that is directly usable.

**How to apply:** the surrounding sentence stays in Russian and the name stays in French, inflected by position rather than by ending — «встреча с Camille Ernould», «в офисе UITH в Tourcoing». The same holds for any language whose names he will need to type back verbatim. Related: [[feedback-no-ciphers-in-chat]], which is the same principle from the other side — write what the reader can act on, not a code they must decode.
