Do not measure a piece of work in human hours, and never argue that something is too large to take on because it "is sixty hours" or "is unliftable as a whole". All implementation in these projects is carried out by agents, not by a person sitting down to do it, so a headcount-hours figure describes nothing that actually constrains the work.

**Why:** Alex reacted sharply to me calling an unfinished plan a "sixty-hour monolith" — «У нас нет уже шестидесяти часовых монолитов. У нас вся работа выполняется агентами, а не людьми» (2026-08-04). The phrase was doing real argumentative work in my recommendation, which made it worse than a stray word: I was recommending that a plan be split, and the stated reason was its size in a unit that no longer applies to anything. A recommendation resting on an obsolete measure is a bad recommendation even when its conclusion happens to be right.

**How to apply:** when arguing that a piece of work should be split, name the constraint that actually binds. How much of the subject one agent can hold at once and still act correctly; how many separate files two agents would collide on if run in parallel; whether the piece has acceptance criteria at its own level so it can be accepted on its own rather than only as part of the whole; and — the usual real reason — how much of the work is on the path to the thing being asked for and how much is unrelated to it. Those are the arguments. Elapsed human time is not one of them, and neither is "it is big".

The same applies to how work is described back to Alex in chat and written into any card or plan. If a document carries an hours field inherited from the older process, raise the question of what that field should now mean instead of quietly filling it with a number that pretends to be a person's day.
