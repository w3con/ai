When a letter is written in English, remember who reads it. Alex is not a native English speaker, and neither are most of his French correspondents. Write at an intermediate level: common words, short sentences, one idea per sentence, no long subordinate chains and no literary flourishes. Not baby English, but nothing that needs a second read.

**Why:** Alex said so on 2026-08-06 about a letter to a French contact. A sentence the writer finds elegant costs a non-native reader real effort, and effort is exactly what makes a letter go unanswered.

**How to apply:** prefer the plain verb over the elaborate one, break a sentence with two subordinate clauses into two sentences, and cut the appositive phrases that only add colour. This composes with [[feedback-cut-slop-keep-depth]]: simplify the sentences, keep the substance. The em-dash ban in outgoing letters still applies, see [[feedback-no-em-dash-in-letters]].
