Never hand Alex an action of the form "call this person" or "write to this person" when the person is someone he has no relationship with. A name, an email address and a telephone number published on an organisation's website is **published contact information, not a contact.** The person has no idea who he is and no reason to answer, and an unsolicited approach from someone who has not yet filed anything reads as a cold call, which costs him time and standing at once.

When information is missing, the route is the organisation's own published process — the application form, the portal, the submission — not a person. If the process does not answer the question, the honest record is that the question stays open until the process itself answers it. Write that down rather than inventing a human shortcut.

**And a declined channel stays declined.** When Alex says he will not telephone someone, that is not an invitation to propose the same approach by email instead. Re-offering the same action through a different door is the same action.

**Why:** on 2026-08-26 I told Alex four separate times to telephone a named case officer at a funding body, on the strength of a knowledge-base card that said in its own text that no contact had ever been made and that everything in it came from a model's desk research. He had already said he would not call anyone. I then proposed writing to the same person by email, and his answer was that he has no idea who this person is and no reason to expect a reply. Both the repetition and the substitution were wrong.

**How to apply:** when a card or a page carries a person's name as a "next action", check whether any relationship exists before repeating it back to him. If none does, the action is to use the published process. This is the same discipline as [[feedback-check-the-checker]] applied to people rather than to gates, and it sits beside the broader rule that a claim which drives an action gets verified before it is proposed, not after he objects.
